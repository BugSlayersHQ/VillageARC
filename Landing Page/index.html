The Core Challenge

Land records were not built for the digital age.

Across India, historical land records continue to exist in the form of handwritten registers, scanned documents, cadastral maps, and legacy PDF files maintained across various administrative levels.

Handwritten Registers & Faded Text

Decades-old physical registers frequently suffer from faded ink, damaged pages, and varying handwriting styles that impede standard transcription.

Poor Image Quality & Scanned PDFs

Scanned legacy documents often contain noise, skew, low resolution, stains, and handwritten annotations that break conventional OCR engines.

Multiple Indian Regional Languages

Land records exist across multiple regional scripts and languages with complex localized terminology, requiring specialized multilingual recognition.

Inconsistent Document Formats

Formats differ across states, tehsils, and historical eras—lacking standardized layouts for Khasra, Khatauni, Jamabandi, and deed documents.

Manual Data Entry & Inconsistencies

Manual data entry is time-consuming, expensive, and error-prone, introducing data inconsistencies that affect decision-making and land governance.

Ownership Verification & Disconnected Systems

The lack of standardized digital records creates hurdles in verifying ownership, resolving disputes, and integrating with modern land information systems.

Proposed Operational Architecture

How the system works from intake to integration.

A structured 6-stage lifecycle designed to convert unstandardized legacy land records into verified, modern digital datasets.

01 Upload Scanned PDFs & maps
02 AI Recognition OCR & Computer Vision
03 Extraction NLP/ML field mapping
04 Validation Business rules & checks
05 Verification Human-in-the-loop
06 Integration LRMS & GIS platforms
STAGE 01

Document Upload

Upload scanned PDFs, images, handwritten registers, cadastral maps, and historical land-record documents through a user-friendly interface.

Input formats: Scanned PDF, TIFF, PNG, JPEG, Cadastral sheets
STAGE 02

AI Recognition

Advanced OCR and Computer Vision analyze document layouts, recognize printed and handwritten text in multiple Indian languages, and process visual features.

Capabilities: Multilingual character recognition, layout segmentation
STAGE 03

Intelligent Extraction

Natural Language Processing and Machine Learning classify recognized data into predefined fields such as landowner, survey number, khasra, khata, and plot area.

Output: Structured land attributes mapped to predefined schemas
STAGE 04

Automated Validation

The validation engine applies predefined business rules, performs cross-database verification against master databases, detects duplicates, and checks consistency.

Validation: Rule consistency, master database verification, duplicate checks
STAGE 05

Human Verification

Fields with low confidence or flagged ambiguities are automatically routed to revenue officials for manual review and human-assisted verification.

Governance: Official review queue, side-by-side image comparison
STAGE 06

Digital Integration

Verified, structured records are designed for proposed integration with LRMS, DILRMP databases, GIS platforms, cadastral maps, and other government databases via APIs.

Proposed Integration: RESTful APIs, GraphQL, GIS spatial integration

Predefined Data Schema

12 Predefined Land-Record Fields

The proposed system automatically extracts and classifies information into the twelve predefined fields explicitly specified in the project study document.

FIELD 01

Landowner Details

Name and details of title holders, co-sharers, parentage, and legal identification references.

FIELD 02

Survey Number

Official revenue survey identification assigned during administrative land surveys.

FIELD 03

Khasra Number

Specific plot or parcel identifier representing land subdivisions within the revenue boundary.

FIELD 04

Khata Number

Account ledger identifier grouping landholdings belonging to a single title holder or family unit.

FIELD 05

Plot Area

Land measurement recorded in standardized metric units and legacy regional measurement units.

FIELD 06

Village

Revenue village (Mauza) jurisdiction indicating the local administrative origin of the parcel.

FIELD 07

Tehsil

Sub-district administrative revenue division (Taluka/Tehsil) overseeing the village records.

FIELD 08

District

Primary district revenue jurisdiction encompassing the sub-district administrative boundaries.

FIELD 09

Land Classification

Official categorization of the parcel (e.g. agricultural, irrigated, residential, or commercial).

FIELD 10

Ownership Details

Tenure status, occupant classification, shareholding proportions, and registered rights.

FIELD 11

Mutation Records

Historical record of title transfers, inheritance, succession entries, partitions, and amendments.

FIELD 12

Registration Information

Deed registration references, document numbers, dates, and sub-registrar office details.

Human-Assisted Verification

AI handles the scale. Humans handle the uncertainty.

The proposed system does not blindly accept every extracted value. It assesses confidence, detects potential discrepancies, and routes uncertain fields for manual verification.

Automated Validation

Predefined business rules, cross-database verification against master registries, and duplicate detection check consistency across all extracted records.

Confidence Scoring & Flagging

Every extracted field receives a qualitative confidence evaluation. Uncertain characters, ambiguous handwriting, or faded text are automatically flagged.

Human-Assisted Verification

Revenue officials review flagged values in an intuitive side-by-side interface showing original scans and extracted data, ensuring full administrative accountability.
